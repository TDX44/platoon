Duty rosters, fairness and the conflict nobody caught

September 21, 2026

A duty roster exists so details land evenly and nobody can argue they were singled out. It fails two ways. Somebody draws CQ twice in a month while somebody else draws none, and somebody gets posted for a night they are on leave.

The second failure is the expensive one

An uneven roster makes a soldier grumble. A roster that posts someone who is on leave produces a 1900 phone call, a scramble for a replacement, and a staff duty NCO who finds out at 2100 that his relief is in another state.

It is also the easier one to prevent, because the unit already has the information. The leave was approved. The TDY was scheduled. The conflict exists the moment the name goes on the roster, and nothing checks.

Fairness is a count, not a feeling

DA Form 6 is built around a simple idea: keep a record of who has done what, and go down the list. That works when the record is current and everyone reads the same copy.

What breaks it is the exceptions. A soldier gets skipped for a valid reason, then skipped again because the person building next month's roster does not know the first skip was temporary. Three months later the platoon is certain the roster is rigged, and they are not entirely wrong.

Check the roster against the absences, before publishing

If leave, pass, TDY and school are already recorded as date windows, a conflict check is a comparison, not a meeting. Every name on the duty roster gets tested against every absence window covering that date. Any overlap is flagged before the roster is published rather than discovered the evening it matters.

This is the practical argument for keeping absences and duty in the same system. Two systems cannot check each other, so a human has to, and the human is doing it at 2200 the night before.

Publish who is eligible, not just who is picked

A roster is easier to defend when the people on it can see the pool it came from. If a platoon can see that eleven soldiers were available on the 14th and the detail went to the one who had gone longest without, the argument ends before it starts.

That also makes gaps obvious. A squad that is never eligible because three of its people are permanently at school is a manning problem, not a fairness problem, and it should show up as one.

The short version

  • Keep absences as dated windows, not status words.
  • Run the duty roster against those windows before it is published.
  • Record why someone was skipped, so the next roster knows it was temporary.
  • Let people see the eligible pool, not only the assignment.
